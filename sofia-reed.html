Design Philosophy

Architecture
in service of
people.

I don't design buildings that look good in photographs and fail the people who use them. My practice begins with the body moving through space — how light falls at seven in the morning, how sound travels at a family dinner, how a corridor makes you feel when you walk it on the worst day of your year.

Trained at the Architectural Association in London and the ETH Zürich, I work primarily in residential, cultural, and small commercial typologies, from a single family home to a community library. Scale matters less than intention.

I
Material Honesty

Concrete is concrete. Timber is timber. We do not disguise one thing as another. Materials are chosen for their genuine properties and the way they age.

II
Light as Material

Every window is a design decision with consequences throughout the day and across the seasons. We model light before structure.

III
The Long View

Buildings must outlast trends. We design for 50 years of useful life, choosing finishes, details, and proportions that endure.

IV
Context

Every building is a conversation with its site — the topography, the climate, the neighbours, the history. We listen before we draw.
